# The Hidden Calories in Restaurant Meals (And How to Spot Them)

Eating out while trying to stick to a nutritional goal can feel like a minefield. You order what looks like a healthy grilled chicken salad, only to find out later that the dressing, oils, and croutons pushed the meal over 1,000 calories. So how do you track macros accurately when you don't have the recipe?

The biggest culprits in restaurant meals are cooking fats and portion distortion. A tablespoon of olive oil contains 120 calories, and most restaurant chefs use significantly more than that to make your food taste incredible. When manually tracking, most people log "grilled chicken" and completely miss the 300 calories of hidden butter it was cooked in.

This is where AI tracking tools shine. Advanced AI doesn't just see "chicken"—it recognizes the sheen of cooking oils, the specific type of sauce, and the realistic portion size of the plate. By snapping a photo of your restaurant meal, you get a much more realistic estimate of those hidden calories, allowing you to enjoy your night out without ruining your weekly progress.
